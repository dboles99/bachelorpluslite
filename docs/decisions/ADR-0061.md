# ADR-0061: Clipboard History Leaves, And Its Policy Axis Goes With It

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-08-29
**Implements:** [ADR-0059](ADR-0059.md) R2
**Amends:** [ADR-0010](ADR-0010.md), [ADR-0020](ADR-0020.md), [ADR-0059](ADR-0059.md)

## Context

[ADR-0059](ADR-0059.md) put `bp-clipboard` second: 946 lines, one dependant,
and one dependency -- `bp-security`, for whether a history may persist.
Windows Notepad has no clipboard history and neither does a note-organising
layer.

It is the first removal in this sequence that touches the **action-id
windows**, which is why it was placed early rather than late. `CLIP_BASE`
(300) and `CLIP_TRANSFORM_BASE` (500) were two of the seven range-dispatch
windows guarded by `no_two_range_dispatch_windows_overlap` -- the test that
holds `CLAUDE.md`'s trap 1, where a range arm silently swallows another
family's ids.

## Decision

**Remove `bp-clipboard`, its two menu families, and the `Clipboard` policy
axis.**

Deleted: the crate, both action-id blocks and `CLIP_TRANSFORM_SLOTS`, the
`clip_end` and `clip_transform_end` bounds, `transform_label`,
`apply_transform`, `decode_transform`, both dispatch arms, the Edit menu's
history section, the polling timer in `lib.rs`, `AppState::clips`, and the
two range-dispatch windows.

**Cut, Copy and Paste stay, and are untouched.** They are `bp_editor::Command`
values over the OS clipboard through `arboard`; `bp-clipboard` was the
*history*, which is a different thing that shared a word.

### The policy axis goes now, not at R5

**This departs from ADR-0059's sequencing, deliberately.** That ADR listed
`Clipboard` among the axes R5 would drop, with the reason *"its subject leaves
under R2"*. Waiting would have left a `Policy` axis governing a crate that no
longer exists, reported by the Security Inspector as **the policy in force** --
which is the exact defect ADR-0059 had just finished diagnosing in four other
axes. **An axis outlives its subject by nothing**, and the session that
removes the subject is the only session that will certainly remember why.

So `bp_security::Clipboard` is gone, along with its field on `Policy`, its
term in `is_at_least_as_strict_as`, its clamp in `Privacy::apply`, its row in
all four named profiles, and its readouts in the Security menu and the Tools
inspector. `Policy` has six axes now, of which **two govern something.**

## Consequences

**Trap 1's guard is exercised rather than weakened.** Two of seven windows
left; `no_two_range_dispatch_windows_overlap` still holds the remaining five
and was not touched. Removing a window is the safe direction -- the failure it
guards is a range that grows into a neighbour -- but the ids 300--499 and
500--599 are now free, and **a later block must not be placed there casually**:
the comment explaining why `CLIP_BASE` sat above 100 has been rewritten to
state the rule (Slint routes `UNDO..=SELECT_ALL` itself) rather than to point
at a constant that no longer exists.

**Four tests were rewritten rather than deleted, and that was the whole risk
of this change.** Each asserted something true whose subject had left:

- `an_empty_clipboard_history_says_so_rather_than_showing_nothing` becomes
  `the_edit_menu_offers_no_clipboard_history`, asserting absence *and* that
  Paste survives -- because the readout row and the history rows shared a code
  path, and a partial removal would have left the menu saying "nothing copied
  yet" forever;
- `the_menu_states_what_the_profile_actually_does` checked three readouts and
  now checks two. Its subject is that the menu *states* what a profile does,
  and one readout would have made it a claim about a row rather than about the
  menu;
- `the_security_inspector_names_all_seven_axes` becomes
  `..._names_every_axis_the_policy_has`, and now also asserts the inspector
  does **not** mention the removed one;
- `privacy_mode_clears_the_clipboard_and_removes_journals` becomes
  `privacy_mode_removes_journals_already_written`. Privacy Mode still has to
  *act* rather than only govern future writes; the journal is now the only
  already-written state it clears, which makes that test **more** load-bearing
  than it was.

**One test would have passed while asserting nothing, and that is the finding
worth keeping.** `a_custom_policy_is_not_forced_into_the_monotonic_chain`
builds a policy stricter than Standard on one axis and *more permissive* on
another, then asserts the two are incomparable. The more-permissive half was
`Clipboard::Persistent`. Delete the axis and the policy becomes strictly
stricter than Standard -- so `is_at_least_as_strict_as` returns `true`, the
`assert!(!...)` fails, and the fix that first suggests itself is to weaken the
assertion.

The axis had to be **replaced**, not dropped: `Embeddings::Cloud` is more
permissive than Standard's `Local`, so the policy is genuinely incomparable
again. Had the test been written with a tolerant assertion it would have gone
green on its own and nobody would have looked.

> **A test that removing a field makes *fail* is doing its job. A test that
> removing a field makes *pass* has stopped.** Both look identical in a diff,
> and only the first one tells you.

**`Policy` is now two-thirds decorative, and R5 inherits a sharper problem.**
Six axes, two enforcing readers. ADR-0059 already recorded that; what this
change adds is that the ratio got worse by removing an axis that *did* govern
something. The inspector's own doc comment now says which three govern nothing
and that they leave under R5 -- because a readout reporting an unenforced axis
is a claim the code does not keep, and saying so in the comment is the least
this can do until R5 removes them.

**R5 will have to replace that incomparability axis again.** After the
security stack goes, `Policy` is `{recovery, metadata}` and Standard is the
most permissive value on both -- so no policy can be incomparable *with
Standard*. It stays constructible against Private, which is stricter on
metadata and can be undercut on recovery. Named here because the test will
break at R5 for a reason that is not obvious from its failure.

**ADR-0010 is superseded and specs.md section 14 goes with it.** Clipboard
history, kind detection and format-aware paste were a designed feature that
shipped and worked; they are removed because the product is a different one
now, not because anything was wrong with them.
