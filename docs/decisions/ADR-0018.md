# ADR-0018: A Custom Editor View, Introduced Opt-In

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-08-17

## Context

Slint's `TextInput` owns its own text, caret and undo stack. It hands the
entire buffer back on every edit, keeps a second undo history over the same
document, and exposes the caret only through a property its own source marks
*"internal, undocumented, only exposed for tests"*.

That one fact was blocking four separate things at once:

- the rope in `bp-buffer` could not become the editor's storage, because a
  rope behind that widget means converting to `String` on every keystroke —
  worse than the `String` it would replace;
- `bp-editor`'s transaction-based undo could not become the authority, and two
  undo stacks over one document is worse than one;
- the status bar could not show Ln/Col, which specs.md section 3 asks for;
- everything in specs.md section 4 that needs a caret — multi-cursor, column
  selection, folding, go-to-line — had nowhere to start.

The large-file engine (phase 4) sits behind the first of those.

Against that, `TextInput` does three things well that a hand-written view does
not get for free: word wrap, input-method composition for CJK entry, and
double- and triple-click selection.

## Decision

**1. Write a custom editor view**, `EditorSurface` in `bp-ui/ui/app.slint`. It
draws only the visible lines, routes keys through a `FocusScope`, and owns
nothing: every decision is made in `bp-editor` and handed to it.

**2. Put the decisions in library crates, not the widget.** Caret motion,
word boundaries, the key-to-command mapping and the document-to-screen
geometry are all in `bp-editor`, tested without a window. The widget draws
what it is given and reports rows, columns and key text back.

**3. Make the rope the storage immediately, for both views.** `bp-ui` holds
`HashMap<DocumentId, bp_editor::Editor>` whichever view is drawing. Text
arriving from `TextInput` lands as a single undoable replacement.

**4. Ship the view opt-in, behind `--editor-view`,** until it reaches parity.

## Rationale

Splitting the decisions from the drawing is what makes the exercise
tractable. A text editor built entirely inside a widget can only be checked by
using it; the same editor with its rules in a library can be checked by
running `cargo test`, and what remains needing a human is the part that is
genuinely visual — does the caret land under the pointer, does the wheel
scroll the right way.

Making the rope the storage for both views was the decision that avoided two
sources of truth. The alternative — keeping `HashMap<DocumentId, String>` and
syncing an `Editor` alongside it — would have meant two representations of one
document, which is the defect this ADR exists to remove, reintroduced for
convenience.

Shipping opt-in is the part worth arguing about. The case for switching
outright is that two paths are a maintenance surface and a hedge. The case
against is that the switch trades three things that work today for a view that
has never been used: word wrap, IME composition, and double-click selection.
Losing CJK input entirely, in a product whose tests are careful about
multibyte text throughout, is not a reasonable price for a view nobody has
typed into yet. The flag is meant to be temporary and should be removed in
either direction rather than kept.

## Consequences

- **Word wrap and IME composition do not work under `--editor-view`.** Both
  are parity conditions for making it the default. Soft wrap in particular is
  not a small addition: it makes a visual line differ from a document line,
  which every function in `bp_editor::view` currently assumes away.
- **Ln/Col works, and only under the new view.** Elsewhere the status bar
  keeps reporting the line count, which is the honest answer when the caret is
  not legitimately readable.
- **Undo differs between the two views**, because under `TextInput` Slint's
  stack is still the one the user drives. Under the surface, `bp-editor`'s
  transactions are the authority, which is the behaviour specs.md describes.
- **The two views must be kept in step** for as long as both exist. The menu
  rows for undo, cut, copy, paste and select-all now route to Slint or to Rust
  depending on the flag, which is the sharpest edge of carrying both.
- The character advance is measured from a hidden probe `Text` rather than
  assumed from the font size. A hard-coded ratio drifts the caret further from
  its character with every column; the measurement is exact for a monospace
  font, and the ratio survives only as a fallback for the frame before layout
  has run.
- **What the tests cannot cover still needs a person**: whether keys arrive,
  whether the caret is drawn where the click was, and which way the wheel
  scrolls. The pure layer is tested; the widget has been shown to render a
  frame without panicking, and no more than that.

## Re-asked and reaffirmed, 2026-08-22 (D2)

**The flag stays opt-in, unchanged.** This ADR says the flag "is meant to be
temporary and should be removed in either direction rather than kept", so the
question of when to remove it has stood open as D2 across six sessions. Asked
again and answered: not yet, and the two conditions for re-asking are worth
restating because neither has been met.

**Parity is not reachable, and that was checked in Slint's source rather than
assumed.** `FocusScope` returns `EventResult::Reject` for `UpdateComposition`
and `CommitComposition` in *both* `capture_key_event` and `key_event`
(`i-slint-core/items/input_items.rs`), and exposes no callback for either.
`TextInput` is the only item in the toolkit that consumes them, and
`input_method_request` is a backend-internal trait rather than an application
hook. **There is no route to pre-edit text from a `FocusScope` on 1.17.1**, so
removing the flag by making the surface the default would drop CJK input
outright -- the precise trade the Rationale above refuses.

Word wrap is no longer on that list: `bp_editor::wrap` owns the breaking rule
and the surface moves by visual row. Double- and triple-click selection landed
too. **Of the three things `TextInput` did better, one is left**, and it is
the one blocked upstream.

**The tax is measured, and it fell.** The cost of carrying both views is the
count of features that work in only one, and on 2026-08-22 that went from five
to four -- not by building anything, but by finding that **Go to Line was on
the list by mistake**. It needs the caret *moved*, which
`set-selection-offsets` does perfectly well; only *reading* the caret is
impossible under `TextInput`. `MENU_MAP.md` had conflated the two.

So the re-ask conditions are unchanged: **revisit when Slint ships composition
support for `FocusScope`, or when the tax becomes visible.** It has just gone
down, which is the opposite.
