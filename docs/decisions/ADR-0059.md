# ADR-0059: What The Reduction Removes, In What Order, And What It Forces

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-08-29
**Scopes:** [ADR-0057](ADR-0057.md), and every removal after it

**This ADR removes nothing.** It decides *what* and *in what sequence*. Each
capability leaves under its own ADR, gated and revertible on its own.

## Context

BachelorPlusLite is a clone of BachelorPad+ being reduced to the feature set
of Windows 11 Notepad, plus a note-organising layer that survives on purpose.
[ADR-0057](ADR-0057.md) took the first capability out. This ADR exists because
of what that one cost: **the code came out in one pass and nineteen files of
record still described it**, and the sweep was affordable only because the two
crates depended on nothing. The removals left are not all that shape.

The product today is **67,316 lines of Rust across 23 crates**. Windows 11
Notepad has tabs, find and replace, word wrap, zoom, a font setting, dark
mode, session restore, Go To, encoding and line-ending selection, spell check
and markdown formatting. Measured against that:

| Group | Crates | Lines | In Notepad? |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Editor core | `bp-core`, `bp-buffer`, `bp-editor`, `bp-files`, `bp-config`, `bp-theme`, `bp-search`, `bp-history`, `bp-ui` | ~34,700 | yes |
| Security stack | `bp-crypto`, `bp-integrity`, `bp-audit`, `bp-redaction`, `bp-secrets` | 13,742 | no |
| Policy model | `bp-security` | 622 | no -- but it is what governs the note layer, so it is **kept**; see section 4 |
| Research | `bp-research` | 5,208 | no |
| Structured data | `bp-data`, `bp-formats` | 2,846 | no |
| Note layer | `bp-storage`, `bp-semantic`, `bp-naming` | 3,217 | no -- kept on purpose |
| Clipboard | `bp-clipboard` | 946 | no |
| Platform | `bp-platform` | 5,762 | partly |

## Decision

### 1. What goes

Eight crates leave, plus one capability that is not a crate. A ninth --
`bp-security` -- is kept and narrowed rather than removed, for the reason
section 4 gives:

| # | Removal | Lines | Why |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| R1 | `bp-research` | 5,208 | Citations, BibTeX, DOI and arXiv identifiers. The furthest thing from Notepad in the workspace, and it depends on nothing |
| R2 | `bp-clipboard` | 946 | Clipboard history and format-aware paste. Neither Notepad nor note-organising |
| R3 | `bp-data` | 2,002 | JSON/YAML/CSV validate, format, minify, convert; the eight-row Data menu |
| R4 | Huge-file mode | ~1,600 in `bp-ui`, plus parts of `bp-buffer` and `bp-search` | The large-file engine, the read-only viewer and `StreamSearch` (ADR-0027, ADR-0030, ADR-0042) |
| R5 | The security stack, **less the part that governs** | 13,742 | `bp-crypto`, `bp-integrity`, `bp-audit`, `bp-redaction`, `bp-secrets`. **`bp-security` is retained and narrowed** -- see section 4 |

With the `bp-ui` modules that go with them -- most of `state/security.rs`
(2,824), `state/data.rs` (498), `state/citations.rs` (365), `viewer.rs` (801),
`audit.rs` (245), `passphrase.rs` (141) and `state/encryption.rs` (519) --
**the workspace goes from 67,316 lines to roughly 40,000.**

### 2. What stays, and the note layer stays whole

`bp-storage`, `bp-semantic` and `bp-naming` all survive, and so does every
surface over them: **the Organize menu entire** (Related Notes, Duplicate
Detection, Suggested Folder), **the Note menu entire** (title, keywords,
summary, outline, statistics, tags, recovery checkpoints), and the three
Research menu rows that read the *store* rather than a bibliography --
Research Report, Open Questions, What the Store Holds (ADR-0041, ADR-0046).

**The Research menu is split, not deleted.** `bp-research` is scholarly
citation infrastructure; Research Report is aggregate reads over `bp-storage`
written by `state/research.rs`. They share a menu and nothing else, which is
what [ADR-0044](ADR-0044.md) established when it declined to make them one
thing. R1 takes Citation Metadata, Find Identifiers and Check Bibliography;
the other three rows do not notice.

### 3. The order, and the reason for it

**R1, R2, R3, R4, R5 -- least entangled first, and R5 last by a wide margin.**

This is ADR-0057's ordering rule applied again: it went first *because*
neither crate depended on anything, which made the sweep affordable and taught
the pattern. The same logic ranks the rest.

- **`bp-research` depends on nothing and nothing depends on it.** The largest
  single win available for the least risk, and the right place to confirm that
  ADR-0057's documentation cost was a property of this repository rather than
  of that one change.
- **`bp-clipboard`** has one dependant (`bp-ui`) and one dependency
  (`bp-security`, for whether history may persist). Small -- and it retires
  one of `bp-security`'s dependants before R5 has to deal with them.
- **`bp-data`** has one dependant. It leaves `bp-formats` alive but narrower;
  see section 5.
- **Huge-file mode is the first removal that is not a crate.** It is a
  capability spread across three crates and the shell, so it is where the
  sweep technique gets tested on something with no directory to delete.
- **The security stack is last** because it is the only removal that forces
  decisions in crates that are staying.

### 4. `bp-security` is kept, because it is the off switch

**The first draft of this ADR removed the whole stack, and that was wrong.**
`bp-security` is not a security *feature*; it is the thing that decides
whether the kept features record anything. Deleting it while keeping the note
layer whole would have left a metadata store that records every save with
nothing able to decline it -- **the reduction would have increased what the
product knows about its user.** The off switch stays.

`bp-security` is not a leaf. Five crates depend on it and three of them are
staying:

```text
bp-security ──> bp-core        a Document carries a profile
            ──> bp-history     whether the recovery journal is written
            ──> bp-storage     whether metadata is recorded at all
            ──> bp-clipboard   leaves under R2
            ──> bp-audit       leaves with the stack
```

#### What is kept, and it is a minority of the crate

`Policy` has seven axes. **Four of them govern nothing.** Counting every
reader in the workspace that is not the definition itself and not
`state/inspectors.rs` -- which only *prints* a policy:

| Axis | Enforcing readers | Verdict |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `recovery` | the journal, in `bp-ui/state.rs` | **kept** |
| `metadata` | `bp-storage`, via `state/organize.rs` | **kept** |
| `clipboard` | `bp-ui` only | **removed** -- its subject leaves under R2 |
| `network` | 1, and it is a readout | **removed** |
| `temporary_files` | 0 | **removed** |
| `zeroise` | 0 | **removed** |
| `embeddings` | 0 | **removed** |

**Four axes exist only to be displayed**, which is trap 3 wearing a type: a
policy the Security Inspector reports as in force, that nothing consults
before acting. `temporary_files` is the sharpest case -- ADR-0048 cited it as
the reason Markdown preview cannot hand HTML to a browser, and that reasoning
is sound, but it is a reason not to *build* something rather than a rule
anything enforces. **A decision recorded in a type is still a decision**
(ADR-0056's lesson); a decision recorded in a type that nothing reads is a
comment with a `pub` on it.

So R5 keeps, from `bp-security`:

- **`Profile`** -- Standard, Private, Confidential, Maximum. The named ceiling,
  and the thing a user actually picks;
- **`Privacy`** -- the session override that can only tighten, with the
  monotonicity guarantee ADR-0020 built and a test holds;
- **`Recovery`**, narrowed. `Encrypted` leaves with `bp-crypto`; `Plaintext`
  and `Disabled` remain, so session restore is still declinable;
- **`Metadata`** -- Summary, PathOnly, Disabled. Whole, because this is the
  store's off switch and the whole reason this section exists.

And it keeps, from `state/security.rs`, only the profile rows and the Privacy
Mode toggle -- a few dozen lines out of 2,824.

#### What R5 still has to decide

1. **The recovery journal stops being encrypted.** [ADR-0022](ADR-0022.md)
   seals it with `bp-crypto` under the document's own passphrase for Private
   and Confidential. With crypto gone, `Recovery::Encrypted` has no
   implementation. **It must not silently become `Plaintext`** -- ADR-0020 is
   explicit that a control which quietly weakens itself is worse than an
   absent one, and `Policy::needs_encryption` exists to make that fail loudly.
   The honest move is to delete the variant so the profiles that asked for it
   now ask for `Disabled` instead: *no journal* rather than *a journal you were
   told was encrypted*.
2. **Existing `.bpadx` documents become unopenable by this build**, with no
   migration path once `bp-crypto` is gone. Whether that wants a final release
   that can still decrypt, a documented "decrypt before upgrading", or nothing
   at all is R5's own ADR's to take rather than inherit.
3. **Whether the menu is still called Security.** What survives is four
   profiles, Privacy Mode and two readouts -- roughly seven rows where there
   are thirteen. With no encryption, signing, secrets or redaction behind it,
   *Privacy* describes the remainder better than *Security* does. A rename is
   cheap now and expensive after somebody documents the old name again.

**These are why R5 is last.** None is hard; all are invisible until somebody
looks, and looking is cheaper once the workspace is 40% smaller.

### 5. What R3 does not remove

`bp-formats` stays. It identifies a file type, and two kept things need that:
`.ipynb` recognition, which [ADR-0057](ADR-0057.md) deliberately preserved,
and `bp-platform`'s registration table, which a test holds in agreement with
it so the product never offers to open a type it cannot identify.

What `bp-formats` loses is `has_data_operations` and the `Profile` values only
the Data menu read. **It becomes a smaller crate with the same job**, which is
a different outcome from `bp-clipboard`'s -- named here so that nobody deletes
it later as vestigial.

## Consequences

**Every removal is one ADR, one gate run, one commit.** Batching was
considered and refused. ADR-0057's diff was 8,229 deletions across 84 files
and it stayed reviewable because it was one idea. Five ideas in one commit is
not five times harder to review, it is worse than that -- and the record sweep
is the part that benefits least from batching, because each capability has its
own nineteen files.

**The sweep is the work, and it is now a known quantity.** That ratio should
be assumed for R1 through R5 rather than rediscovered, and the places to look
are already enumerated by ADR-0057: `specs.md`, `ROADMAP.md`'s phase table,
`MENU_MAP.md`, `ARCHITECTURE.md`'s crate map and module table,
`SECURITY_MODEL.md`, `PLATFORM_MATRIX.md`, `DATA_MODEL.md`, `fuzz/README.md`,
`LOCAL_CI.md`, `Invoke-LocalCI.ps1`, `.github/workflows/ci.yml`, the rosettas,
`NEXT_SESSION.md`'s manual checklist, `WORK_QUEUE.md`, `README.md` -- and
`CLAUDE.md` itself, which held a non-negotiable about a capability that had
already been deleted.

**Two recorded decisions stop being about anything, one survives narrowed, and
one gets stronger.** [ADR-0021](ADR-0021.md)'s envelope and
[ADR-0024](ADR-0024.md)'s audit log go with R5.
[ADR-0011](ADR-0011.md)'s "no custom cryptography" becomes vacuous exactly as
ADR-0025's never-auto-run did -- there is no cryptography left to implement
badly -- and by the rule ADR-0057 set, **a constraint that cannot fail is
recorded as removed rather than left to read as live.**

**ADR-0020's profile model survives**, which the first draft of this ADR had
it not doing. It survives on two axes instead of seven and without an
encrypted journal, but the shape it argued for is intact: named profiles
ordered by strictness, a session override that can only tighten, and a
monotonicity check that is a walk over consecutive pairs. **That model was
never really about cryptography** -- it is about a user being able to say what
this program may write down, which matters at least as much in a Notepad clone
as it did here. ADR-0020 should be amended rather than superseded.

The one that gets *stronger* is [ADR-0006](ADR-0006.md): core editing never
depends on cloud or AI services. It survives untouched and matters more, not
less, because after R1--R5 the note layer is the only thing left that could
ever want to.

**Phase 10 cannot be taken on the queue's word.** `WORK_QUEUE.md` lists E1, E2
and E3 as ready under [ADR-0056](ADR-0056.md). E3 was already written around
`UserGesture` types ADR-0057 deleted, and semantic search is further from
Notepad than notebooks were. Building it during a reduction needs a decision
rather than an estimate. **This ADR does not take that decision**; it records
that those rows predate the reduction, so a later session does not read them
as approved.

**Deliberately not decided here:** whether `bp-platform`'s default-editor
registration survives, and whether the `--editor-view` fork (ADR-0018, D2) is
still worth its tax at 40,000 lines. Both are real questions. Neither blocks
R1, which is why neither is answered now.

## The lesson

> **A reduction is not automatically a simplification, and the place it stops
> being one is where the deleted thing was somebody's off switch.**

This ADR's first draft removed the security stack entire, and the inventory is
what caught it: keeping the note layer whole while deleting the profile model
would have produced a metadata store that records every save with no way to
decline -- **a smaller product that knows more about its user than the larger
one did.** The fix was not to keep more; it was to ask which parts *govern*
something and keep exactly those.

Asking that question produced the second finding, which nobody was looking
for: **four of `Policy`'s seven axes have no enforcing reader at all.** They
are computed, stored, and displayed by an inspector that reports them as the
policy in force. That is ADR-0048's discovery in a new place -- there, 38
menu rows marked *planned* were mostly finished work nobody had pruned; here,
a security model advertises four guarantees nothing implements. **Both were
found by counting rather than by reading**, and in both cases the count was
available to anyone at any time for the price of one `grep`.
