# Your first note, start to finish

Ten minutes, and it touches most of what makes this product different from
Notepad.

## 1. Open it and type something

Launch `bachelorpad` (or `bpad`). You get an empty *Untitled* document.

Type a few paragraphs with a heading at the top:

```markdown
# Quarterly review notes

Three things came up that need a decision before the end of the month.

## Pricing
...
```

The status bar already shows the format it has recognised, the encoding, the
line ending, and that the document has never been saved.

## 2. Save it somewhere ordinary

`Ctrl+S`, and put it in your Documents folder as `notes.md`.

The status bar now shows the **full path** and the time it was written to
disk. Not when you last typed -- when it last actually reached the disk, which
is the fact you want when you are wondering whether you have lost anything.

## 3. Let it name the file

**Note > Semantic Rename...**

It proposes something like `Quarterly_Review_Notes_10SEP2026.md`, having taken
the title from your heading and the date from today. You see the proposal
before anything happens, and nothing is renamed until you approve it.

That shape -- `Title_DDMMMYYYY.ext` -- means a folder of these sorts
chronologically and reads as prose, which is the whole idea.

## 4. Ask what it knows

**Note > Keywords** shows the terms this document is indexed under.
**Note > Summary** shows a short summary drawn from the text.
**Note > Outline** shows your headings as a structure.
**Note > Document Statistics** counts words, lines and characters.

None of this went anywhere. It is computed on your machine, from the document
in front of you.

## 5. Write a second note, then connect them

Make another note on a related subject and save it. Now, from either one:

**Organize > Related Notes.**

A panel opens listing documents that share tags with this one, most related
first. Click a row and it opens, scrolled to the relevant place.

With two notes this is not impressive. With two hundred it is the reason this
product exists.

## 6. See the shape of the whole collection

**Research > Research Report.**

It reads the index and writes you a report: dominant themes, documents that
are under-connected to everything else, what the store holds.

Read the end of it. **It tells you what it did not look at** -- how many
documents it scanned, how many it capped each section at. A report about
"your notes" that is quietly about some of them is a report you cannot use,
so this one says which.

## 7. Decide what it is allowed to remember

**Privacy.**

Everything in steps 4 to 6 depends on the index, and whether the index records
anything is your choice, per document. The other axis is whether a recovery
journal is written -- the thing that saves your work when the power goes out,
by writing your text to disk before you asked it to.

Both trades are real and neither has a free option. [Privacy](../troubleshooting/privacy.md)
lays them out in a table.
